# Military Calisthenics — Three-Week Foundation

Revised 31 October 2026. Program structure and opening sample approved; exercise variation expanded at the user's request. Video demonstration sample remains pending review. This is an original general-fitness program, not an official military test preparation protocol.

## Onboarding and personalisation

Ask every user before assigning training: adult age range; beginner/returning, regular or advanced experience; strength, general fitness or conditioning/fat-loss-support goal; available equipment; three, four or five training days; current pain, injuries, exercise restrictions, balance limitations and ability to get to/from the floor. Confirm starting exercise variations with a comfortable practice attempt, not a maximum-effort test.

Default equipment: mat, wall and sturdy non-wheeled chair placed against a wall. Never use the chair for jumping, dips or improvised pull-ups. No-equipment users use standing squat regressions and wall support. Resistance-band users can replace prone shoulder exercises with appropriate band rows; pull-up-bar users need a separately calibrated pulling variation. Do not invent unsafe furniture anchors.

Select difficulty per exercise, not just per person. Injury answers require appropriate exclusions and clarification when needed; the app must not pretend to prescribe rehabilitation. Users who cannot get to the floor need a reviewed standing program, not automatic assignment of the floor routine below.

Goals change emphasis, not the time cap: strength prioritises a sufficiently challenging controlled variation and ample recovery; general fitness uses the balanced default; conditioning users may add D/E while retaining the strength sessions. Fat loss is not guaranteed by this program. Military-test preparation needs the actual test requirements and a separate plan.

## Timing and weekly rhythm

Each workout: 2 minutes warm-up + 12 work intervals + 2 minutes cooldown. Warm-up and cooldown count toward the active limit; rests, demonstrations and transitions do not. If a user physically rehearses a demonstration, that movement time must count too. The app must enforce an absolute 15-minute active cap.

| Level | Interval | Main work | Total active time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Beginner/returning | 30 sec | 6 min | 10 min |
| Regular | 40 sec | 8 min | 12 min |
| Advanced | 50 sec | 10 min | 14 min |

An interval is a maximum, not an obligation to keep moving after form breaks. Stop strength sets with approximately 2–3 controlled repetitions in reserve. Do not accelerate repetitions to fill the timer. Rest at least 30 seconds between stations and 60 seconds at the designated midpoint, replacing the usual station rest. Extend rest as needed. With these default rests, elapsed durations are 16, 18 and 20 minutes respectively, before extra transitions or instruction.

Train strength on days 1, 3 and 5 of each week; optional conditioning on days 2 and 6; recover on days 4 and 7. Three-day users do strength only. Four-day users add the first conditioning session. Five-day users add both. Conditioning remains conversational and should not impair the next strength session.

## All three weeks

Six stations are listed in execution order. Each station is performed twice. The opening session is retained exactly; day 19 repeats it to compare technique, perceived effort and comfortable repetitions using the same variations. Other sessions vary exercise selection and emphasis.

### Week 1 — Establish technique

Format: complete all six stations, rest at the midpoint, then repeat all six.

| Day | Session | Six stations |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundation — approved sample | Squat; push-up; glute bridge; prone W raise; dead bug; standing calf raise |
| 2 | March and move — optional | Brisk march; step jack; standing alternating knee drive; side step with reach; straight-punch shadow boxing; alternating heel dig with arm swing |
| 3 | Stability | Supported split squat left; supported split squat right; push-up; standing hip hinge; prone W raise; side plank (left in round 1, right in round 2) |
| 4 | Recovery | Rest; optional easy everyday movement outside the prescribed session |
| 5 | Full-body control | Squat; push-up; glute bridge; prone W raise; bird dog; forearm plank |
| 6 | Coordination — optional | March with opposite-hand knee touch; lateral step with shallow squat; step jack; alternating knee drive; straight-punch shadow boxing; brisk march |
| 7 | Rest | No prescribed workout |

### Week 2 — Add variety and control

Format for strength: paired stations. Perform 1,2,1,2; then 3,4,3,4; then 5,6,5,6. The extended midpoint rest follows work interval 6. Conditioning keeps the week-1 circuit format. This remains 12 intervals, with unchanged active time.

| Day | Session | Six stations |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | Pause and control | Pause squat; push-up; glute bridge march; prone reverse snow angel; supine heel taps; slow calf raise |
| 9 | Footwork — optional | Forward-and-back marching steps; standing hamstring curl with arm swing; step jack; side-to-side toe tap; straight-punch shadow boxing; heel dig with overhead reach |
| 10 | Step-back strength | Alternating reverse lunge; push-up; hip hinge; prone W raise; side plank left; side plank right |
| 11 | Recovery | Rest |
| 12 | Lateral control | Alternating shallow lateral lunge; push-up; glute bridge with brief top pause; prone reverse snow angel; bird dog with brief hold; kneeling plank shoulder tap |
| 13 | Rhythm — optional | March with opposite-hand knee touch; double side step; alternating knee drive; light uppercut shadow boxing; standing hamstring curl; low-impact step jack |
| 14 | Rest | No prescribed workout |

New movements replace existing ones; they do not automatically add difficulty. Beginners may retain the week-1 equivalent. Pairing must not shorten recovery when the user is fatigued.

### Week 3 — Consolidate and compare

Format: two rounds of six stations. Strength users may choose one harder variation only where previous sessions were comfortable and technically controlled. Maintain the same timer and rest options.

| Day | Session | Six stations |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | Controlled strength | Squat with controlled lowering; push-up; bridge march; prone W raise with brief hold; dead bug with longer comfortable reach; side plank (left round 1, right round 2) |
| 16 | Athletic movement — optional | March with deliberate arm drive; lateral step with shallow squat; standing alternating knee drive; forward-and-back marching steps; straight-punch shadow boxing; step jack |
| 17 | Unilateral control | Supported split squat left; supported split squat right; push-up with brief bottom pause; staggered-stance hip hinge (switch halfway); prone reverse snow angel; kneeling plank shoulder tap |
| 18 | Recovery | Rest |
| 19 | Foundation progress check | Squat; push-up; glute bridge; prone W raise; dead bug; standing calf raise — match day-1 variations and timing, without testing to failure |
| 20 | Conditioning mix — optional | Double side step with reach; heel dig with arm swing; light uppercut shadow boxing; standing hamstring curl; march with opposite-hand knee touch; step jack |
| 21 | Rest and review | Record adherence, technique confidence, effort and discomfort; no further weeks generated without the owner's go-ahead |

## Exercise definitions and substitutions

Use controlled movement and comfortable range. A deliberately shortened range is acceptable; worsening or sharp pain is a stop signal. Never hold the breath through a repetition.

| Family | Definition / key cue | Easier choice | Harder choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Squat | Whole foot grounded; bend knees and hips in a comfortable range | Chair sit-to-stand or shallow supported squat | Three-second lowering or short bottom pause |
| Push-up | Maintain a steady trunk; controlled lowering | Wall, then kneeling push-up | Full push-up with slow lowering or brief pause |
| Glute bridge | Lie on back with knees bent; lift hips using glutes without arching | Lower lift | Brief top hold, then bridge march |
| Bridge march | Keep pelvis steady while alternately lifting one foot a little | Ordinary bridge | Slower alternating steps; no need for greater height |
| Prone W raise | Lie face down, elbows bent in W; gently lift arms without shrugging or craning neck | Shoulder-blade squeeze with arms supported | Brief hold; band row if suitably equipped |
| Reverse snow angel | Face down; small controlled arm sweep from near hips toward an overhead position within comfortable shoulder range | Prone W raise or smaller sweep | Slower sweep; avoid forcing overhead range |
| Dead bug | On back; extend opposite arm/leg without lower-back arching | Alternating heel slide | Longer lever only while trunk stays controlled |
| Supine heel tap | On back with hips/knees bent; lower one heel gently toward floor | Heel slide with other foot supported | Longer controlled lowering, not faster taps |
| Calf raise | Rise onto forefeet; lower slowly | Two legs with chair support | Single leg, switching halfway |
| Split squat | Fixed staggered stance; lower vertically, holding chair if needed | Shallow range with support | Slower descent or brief pause |
| Reverse lunge | Step back, lower comfortably, return; alternate evenly | Supported split squat, split interval equally between sides | Greater comfortable range or slow descent |
| Lateral lunge | Step sideways; bend stepping leg while sending hips back | Small side step and shallow squat | Greater comfortable range |
| Hip hinge | Soft knees; hips move back with a long neutral spine | Short range with hands on hips | Staggered stance; switch halfway |
| Side plank | Elbow under shoulder; steady hips | Bent knees | Straight legs; do not progress through shoulder discomfort |
| Bird dog | On hands/knees; reach opposite arm and leg without rotating | One limb at a time | Brief controlled hold |
| Forearm plank | Elbows under shoulders; breathe with a steady trunk | Knees down | Full plank, then slightly longer lever |
| Kneeling shoulder tap | Hands under shoulders, knees down; alternate hand to opposite shoulder without rocking | Shift weight gently without lifting a hand | Full plank shoulder tap with feet wide |
| March / knee drive | Alternate lifting feet or knees; remain upright | Lower lift, chair support if needed | Brisker controlled rhythm |
| Step jack | Step one foot out while arms rise comfortably; return and alternate | Smaller step, arms below shoulder height | Larger controlled steps; jumping is not required |
| Side step / double side step | Step laterally and bring other foot in; double version takes two steps each way | Small step-touch | Add comfortable arm reach |
| Heel dig / toe tap | Lightly place heel in front or toe to the side; alternate | Smaller reach, no arm action | Coordinated arm swing or reach |
| Standing hamstring curl | Bend knee to bring heel toward buttock, alternating; keep knees near each other | Smaller bend with support | Add arm swing and a quicker controlled rhythm |
| Shadow boxing | Light punches with relaxed shoulders; avoid locking elbows | Slower straight punches | Controlled straight punches or small uppercuts; no weights |
| Forward/back marching | Small steps forward and back in clear space | March on the spot | Slightly larger steps with stable balance |

Alternate sides evenly for unilateral alternating movements. For explicitly separate left/right stations, each side receives a full interval. For single-station side planks, side assignment is by round. Demonstrations must state the rule onscreen.

Prone shoulder exercises are not substitutes for progressively loaded rows or pull-ups. A mat-and-chair-only program has a genuine pulling limitation. Bodyweight hinges likewise have limited loading potential for advanced users. Do not market the advanced lane as optimal maximal-strength training without equipment.

## Varied warm-ups and cooldowns

Use warm-up A/B/C in rotation across the five weekly sessions (A, B, C, A, B in week 1; B, C, A, B, C in week 2; C, A, B, C, A in week 3). Each contains four 30-second movements.

- A: easy march; shoulder circles/arm swings; small hip hinges; shallow squat or sit-to-stand.
- B: gentle step-touch; ankle rocks; supported alternating knee lift; shallow supported split-stance knee bends, switching sides after 15 seconds.
- C: heel digs; gentle arm reaches; small side steps; shallow squats.

Cooldown A: slowing march 30 sec; supported calf stretch left 30 sec; right 30 sec; relaxed standing breathing and shoulder movement 30 sec.

Cooldown B: gentle step-touch 30 sec; comfortable standing chest opening 30 sec; calf stretch left 30 sec; right 30 sec. Use A after strength and B after conditioning. Stretches should be mild, not forced.

## Progression and feedback

Week 1 establishes technique. Week 2 adds variation while preserving the original exercise family and approximate effort. Week 3 permits one harder exercise variation where appropriate. Do not also raise speed, shorten recovery and increase range in the same progression.

After every session ask about overall effort, discomfort and exercise difficulty. Offer repeat, easier or harder options. Pain is not a badge, and skipped workouts must not produce catch-up punishment sessions. A missed day resumes the schedule with adequate recovery.

General activity remains useful beyond these brief workouts: this plan alone does not meet the full weekly aerobic-activity recommendation.
